World models and memory for embodied intelligence

I study how robots can use world models to reason about actions and improve through interaction. My research connects action-grounded 4D prediction, video generation, and geometry-grounded memory. My goal is to build physical agents that test alternatives in learned environments and turn real-world feedback into reusable knowledge.

World–action models

Verifiable 4D World–Action Model for Robust Robot Control

X. Wu*, R. Cai*, Y. Bao, et al.

Under review at ICLR 2027Co-first author

Jointly predicting appearance, depth, motion fields, and actions makes a robot’s imagined future geometrically testable. S4NDBOX uses agreement between predicted motion and candidate actions to guide selection before execution, with evaluations on domain-randomized manipulation and zero-shot simulation-to-real transfer.

Spatial reasoning & memory

GaMe: Geometry as Memory for Multi-View Spatial Reasoning

X. Wu, X. Lyu, Y. Yu, et al.

Under review at ICRA 2027First author

GaMe aggregates visual and geometric features across views into a 16-slot working-memory bank. A second language-model pass reads this compact representation alongside the original images and question, bringing geometric evidence into multi-view spatial reasoning.

Long-video generation

VideoSSM: Autoregressive Long Video Generation with Hybrid State-Space Memory

Y. Yu*, X. Wu*, X. Hu, et al.

arXiv preprint, 2025Co-first author

VideoSSM combines a lossless local attention window with recurrent state-space memory for older frames. This hybrid design preserves recent visual detail while retaining long-range context, supporting minute-long autoregressive generation and prompt changes without an ever-growing KV cache.

Paper

Dynamic 3D geometry

EAG3R: Event-Augmented 3D Geometry Estimation for Dynamic and Extreme-Lighting Scenes

X. Wu, Y. Yu, X. Lyu, et al.

NeurIPS 2025 · SpotlightFirst author

SNR-aware fusion weights RGB and event features by image reliability, while event-based photometric constraints refine the recovered geometry. This combination supports depth and camera-motion estimation in dynamic, low-light scenes, including daytime-to-nighttime transfer without nighttime fine-tuning.

Continuous scene perception

LiFR-Seg: Anytime High-Frame-Rate Segmentation via Event-Guided Propagation

X. Wu, X. Lyu, Y. Yu, et al.

ICLR 2026First author

LiFR-Seg updates semantic predictions between RGB frames using asynchronous events. Confidence-aware feature warping and temporal-memory attention preserve previously observed content as the scene evolves, enabling segmentation at a requested time without an RGB frame at that instant.
