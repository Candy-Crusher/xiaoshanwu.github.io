About

World models for generalizable robotic planning.

I’m Xiaoshan Wu （吴小山）.

I am a Ph.D. candidate at the University of Hong Kong, advised by Xiaojuan Qi, and a Visiting Scholar at Harvard University, working with Yilun Du and Mengyu Wang.

I received dual B.S. degrees in Computer Engineering through the ZJUI Institute at Zhejiang University and UIUC.

Research perspective

I am interested in world models for generalizable robotic planning: how predictions of the physical world can support reliable action in unfamiliar environments.

My work develops the perceptual and predictive foundations for this goal, from recovering and retaining spatial evidence to imagining physically plausible 4D futures that guide robot actions and support pre-execution consistency checks. My recent world–action model is evaluated through zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

Looking ahead

I am now interested in agent–world model co-evolution: using task-relevant prediction errors to guide interaction and model refinement, while action-conditioned world models support policy evaluation, improvement, and longer-horizon planning.

A personal inspiration

Joi in Blade Runner 2049 inspires my interest in AI as a presence in everyday life. For me, the research question is how to ground that presence in an understanding of the world, meaningful action, and the ability to learn from experience.
