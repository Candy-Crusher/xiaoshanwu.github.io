About

I am a Visiting Scholar at Harvard University and a fourth-year Ph.D. student at the University of Hong Kong. I received dual B.S. degrees in Computer Engineering from Zhejiang University and UIUC through the ZJUI Institute.

Research perspective

I am interested in how robots can use a coherent understanding of the physical world to act reliably in unfamiliar environments. This requires more than recognizing what is in an image: a robot must recover spatial relationships, retain useful evidence across observations, and anticipate how the world may change.

My work approaches this question through spatial perception, geometric memory, and future prediction. The common goal is to make information about the world useful for action—not simply to produce another image or representation.

Looking ahead

I am now interested in agent–world model co-evolution: using task-relevant prediction errors to guide interaction and model refinement, while action-conditioned world models support policy evaluation, improvement, and longer-horizon planning.

A personal inspiration

Joi in Blade Runner 2049 inspires my interest in AI as a presence in everyday life. For me, the research question is how to ground that presence in an understanding of the world, meaningful action, and the ability to learn from experience.
